What is AI, really?

Spoiler: it's a very, very good guesser.

Transition: before we use AI, let's figure out what it actually is.

Game

Guess the next word.

After school I grabbed a snack and turned on the

Shout it out!

Read the sentence out loud and pause. Let people shout guesses. Collect 4–5 answers. Most people will say TV, Xbox/PlayStation, music… Nobody says "oven". Why? Because you've seen thousands of sentences like this. Your brain predicted the next word.

Click once: bars grow. "A chatbot gives every possible next word a score like this. It picks one (usually a likely one, sometimes a surprising one), adds it to the sentence, and does the whole thing again. Word by word. That's how it writes an essay, a poem, or code." Click again for the takeaway box.

How a chatbot is made

Three steps. No magic.

1

Read

a gigantic amount of text: websites, books, code.

2

Learn patterns

which words tend to follow which, in what situations.

3

Predict

the next word, one at a time, over and over.

It predicts what sounds right — not what is right.
One click per step. Keep it short. Last click is the most important sentence of today: "It predicts what SOUNDS right, not what IS right." Ask them to remember it — we're going to prove it in 20 minutes.

Confident ≠ Correct.

Every session has an AI Checkpoint. Today: catching the AI being confidently wrong.

With your partner · 8 minutes

Pairs use ChatGPT (or whatever AI they're allowed to use). Timer: 8 minutes. LEADERS: look up the real answers the day before from official sources, and swap in fresher questions if these become too easy. Debrief: hands up — who got at least one ✗ or ? ... Ask a pair to read out the most confident wrong answer. Did the AI sound unsure? (Usually no.)

Why does that happen?

Fluent is not the same as true.

“Great question! The Burch Library is open until 9 PM on Saturdays, so you have plenty of time to visit.”

UNVERIFIED

It's guessing words,not looking things up (unless it searches).

Its knowledge has a cutoff,so local + recent facts are weak.

It's trained to sound helpful,so it rarely says “I don't know.”

So you are the fact-checker. Always.
The bubble is an example of a confident answer (we made it up for the slide — it's the kind of thing AIs say). Click: stamp. Then the three reasons. Connect back to slide 7: it predicts what SOUNDS right. This is also why AI-written code can look perfect and still be broken.

How we use AI here

The 4 habits.

1

Explain-back

No AI code goes in your project until you can explain it out loud.

2

Tutor mode

Ask for hints and a plan first. Full code second.

3

Pair up

The beginner types. The partner navigates. Leaders never grab your keyboard.

4

Prompt journal

Write down what you asked, what came back, what broke.

One click per habit. These are the rules every week, not just today. Why? Research on beginners using AI to code: the ones who let AI do everything end up unable to fix or change their own project. The ones who use it like a tutor get better. Hand out the printed habits card.

Non-negotiable · not in prompts, not on your site

✗Your full name
✗Your school
✗Your address
✗Phone numbers
✗Photos of people

And always: an adult checks your site before you share the link.

Use a nickname or first name only on your site. No sign-up forms, no collecting other people's info. If you're not sure, ask a leader.
