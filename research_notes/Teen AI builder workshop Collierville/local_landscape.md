# Local Landscape: Running a Free Teen AI Workshop in Collierville and Greater Memphis (as of Nov 2026)

Method note: ~15 web searches and fetches on 2026-11-10. The colliervilletn.gov pages (library home and policy PDFs) returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch, so Collierville details come from search-result snippets of the town's own documents and the library's event calendar (collierville.libnet.info). Verify every policy detail by phone before pitching. Several sources are from earlier years; these are marked "(dated)".

## 1. Collierville: Lucius E. & Elsie C. Burch Jr. Library

### Takeaway
Burch Library already runs teen programs in its 40-person Halle Room and has a Teen Library Ambassadors group (grades 6-12). That group is the most natural way for students to get involved. The meeting-room policy charges outside organizations, but library-co-sponsored programs are a separate path. I found no written policy on minors proposing programs, so the students should pitch it as a library co-hosted program, not a room rental.

### Cited Findings
- The library is a town department. Its web presence is under colliervilletn.gov/residents/library, and events are listed at collierville.libnet.info. Main phone: 901-457-2601 (appears in event listings, including for laptop requests) — [libnet event listing](https://collierville.libnet.info/event/13986907); [Patron Responsibilities & Conduct](https://www.colliervilletn.gov/residents/library/services/patron-responsibilities-conduct)
- Meeting space: the Halle Room holds about 40 people. Teen programs have been held there, for example a teen defensive driving course and a teen cooking demonstration — [libnet events](https://collierville.libnet.info/event/16121974); [libnet events](https://collierville.libnet.info/event/12877227)
- Room policy (from town-published library documents; exact year unclear): the room can be reserved up to 3 months ahead (2024 application form). Free use is limited to Collierville community book clubs, with library approval. Other businesses and organizations pay $40/hr. Programs must not disrupt library use. Fundraising is allowed only at library- or Friends-sponsored events. No parties or showers. Food is allowed in the Halle Room only during meetings or library programs — [Town library document](https://colliervilletn.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/6089/638568324452570000); [Town library document](https://www.colliervilletn.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/7183/638986224153000000) (403 on direct fetch; details from search snippets)
- Minors: children under 10 must be accompanied by a parent or responsible person — [Patron Responsibilities & Conduct](https://www.colliervilletn.gov/residents/library/services/patron-responsibilities-conduct)
- Teen Library Ambassadors: grades 6-12, Burch library card required. Members give feedback on teen library services and do volunteer tasks. Selection is by staff-reviewed application, not first-come. Meetings are the first Thursday of the month, 5:30-7 p.m. Group size is listed as up to 20 in one listing and up to 24 in another. The fall 2026 application window closed around Aug 9, 2026 — [libnet event](https://collierville.libnet.info/event/14977780); [libnet event](https://collierville.libnet.info/event/16716856)
- Technology: some library classes provide laptops ("laptops will be provided, but you are welcome to bring your own"). Others offer "a limited number of library laptops" and encourage attendees to bring their own device — [libnet event](https://collierville.libnet.info/event/13349571); [libnet event](https://collierville.libnet.info/event/13986879)

### Inferences
- The $40/hr fee is for outside organizations renting the room. A free teen program the library adopts as its own (on its calendar, with registration through libnet) would likely skip the fee. That is the ask to make to teen services staff.
- Joining the Teen Library Ambassadors (next cycle probably opens around summer 2027 for fall) gives the students standing as library volunteers who have suggested a teen program. It is also a ready pool of first participants.
- A 40-person room and a limited laptop pool suggest capping enrollment at about 15-20 and asking participants to bring their own device, with library loaners as backup. Collierville's high device access (Section 5) supports BYOD.

### Gaps
- No written policy found on programs led by outside people or minors, or on whether instructors need a background check. Ask teen services staff.
- No confirmed projector or display in the Halle Room, and no Wi-Fi specs or bandwidth limits (relevant for cloud AI tools). Ask staff.
- No information found on Friends of the Burch Library grants or sponsorships.
- Name of the current teen services librarian not collected (organizational contacts only). Use the main number or the library's contact page.

## 2. Memphis Public Libraries (MPL): CLOUD901, branch teen services, partner pathways

### Takeaway
CLOUD901 at the Central Library (3030 Poplar Ave) is a large, free teen lab for ages 13-18. It is media- and maker-heavy and already hosts outside-led coding workshops, including one led by White Station High School students. That sets a precedent for a teen-led AI workshop. Eight branches have Teen Innovation Centers. Partners reach programming through CLOUD901 staff and the volunteer/mentor process, which requires training and a background check.

### Cited Findings
- CLOUD901 location: 1st and 2nd floors of Benjamin L. Hooks Central Library, 3030 Poplar Ave, Memphis 38111, on the MATA bus line. Ages 13-18 with a library card. Staff, security, volunteers, and mentors are exempt from the age limit. Parents may tour but cannot stay — [CLOUD901 Basics](https://www.memphislibrary.org/cloud901/basics/)
- Spaces and equipment: video and audio production labs, editing and mixing stations, maker space, performance area, learning and collaboration zones, Play Café. High-demand equipment may need a reservation. Some listings mention 3D printers and game consoles — [CLOUD901 Basics](https://www.memphislibrary.org/cloud901/basics/); [CLOUD901 page](https://www.memphislibrary.org/cloud901/)
- Size is reported as about 8,300 sq ft in one source and about 9,000 sq ft in another (conflict) — [High Ground News](https://www.highgroundnews.com/innovationnews/Cloud901Opening.aspx); [StoryBoard Memphis](https://storyboardmemphis.org/community/cloud901/)
- Hours (needs verification): official FAQ snippet gives Mon-Thu 3-8 p.m., Sat 10 a.m.-6 p.m., Fri closed. Older articles conflict. Phone given as 901-415-2760 — [CLOUD901 page](https://www.memphislibrary.org/cloud901/)
- Programs: self-guided learning, expert-led workshops (for example ACT Prep and Coding Video Games), and performances. The Basics page does not mention AI or robotics — [CLOUD901 Basics](https://www.memphislibrary.org/cloud901/basics/). There was a CLOUD901 FIRST Robotics team interest interview — [library calendar](https://memphis.librarycalendar.com/event/cloud901-first-robotics-team-interest-interviews-34010)
- Precedent for a student-led workshop: "White Station High School Presents: Coding Workshop (ages 13-15)," Mon-Fri on the CLOUD901 2nd floor (listing appears to be from 2025) — [library calendar](https://memphis.librarycalendar.com/event/white-station-high-school-presents-coding-workshop-ages-13-15-29709)
- CodeCrew has run camps at MPL (e.g., "Code Crew's Designers & Data: Tech Wearables") — [library calendar](https://memphis.librarycalendar.com/event/code-crew-data-design-camp-9895)
- Mentors and volunteers lead workshops. Prospective volunteers contact CLOUD901 staff about opportunities, training, and background checks. Educators arrange tours through staff. The lab cannot be rented for private events — [CLOUD901 Basics](https://www.memphislibrary.org/cloud901/basics/). Volunteer portal: [volunteer.memphislibrary.org](https://volunteer.memphislibrary.org/vrcnominationform)
- Teen Innovation Centers are at 8 branches: Cordova, East Shelby, Gaston Park, Hollywood, North, Officer Geoffrey Redd, South, Whitehaven. They offer STEAM technology, literacy-based programming, and networking — [Teen Innovation Centers](https://www.memphislibrary.org/teen-innovation-centers/). Coding kits in the "tech cabinet" were noted in 2022 coverage (dated) — [Memphis Parent](https://memphisparent.com/features/memphis-public-libraries-opens-new-teen-innovation-centers/)
- MPL Youth Action Council: teen members can suggest and implement library policies, programming, and resources — [search result summary of MPL pages](https://www.memphislibrary.org/teens) (not independently fetched)
- Innovator-in-Residence program (Memphis Library Foundation and MPL; Cossitt and Raleigh branches) chooses innovators and disciplines based on customer and partner feedback (2025) — [IIR description PDF](https://www.memphislibrary.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Innovator-In-Residence-Opportunity-Description.pdf)
- Outreach page: [MPL Outreach](https://www.memphislibrary.org/outreach/). No public partner-proposal form was found.
- Memphis Library Foundation: a nonprofit supporting MPL. It funded CLOUD901 construction through a $1.9M capital campaign from Memphis businesses, foundations, and individuals. It is credited as sponsor on 2026 CLOUD901 programs, such as a Teen DJ Camp (registration opened May 18, 2026) and Intro to Photography Basics (Nov 2, 2026) — [library calendar](https://memphis.librarycalendar.com/event/cloud901-dj-camp-46952); [From Common Ground](https://fromcommonground.com/?p=24468); [GuideStar](https://www.guidestar.org/profile/62-1590768); [Foundation Substack](https://memphislibraryfoundation.substack.com/p/what-is-a-library-foundation)
- Past CLOUD901 corporate donor: Georgia-Pacific Memphis Cellulose, $25,000 (year not stated) — [From Common Ground](https://fromcommonground.com/?p=23280)
- MPL received an IMLS grant (LG-260696-OLS-26). Its purpose was not reviewed — [IMLS](https://imls.gov/grants/awarded/lg-260696-ols-26)

### Inferences
- Best Memphis entry point: register both students as MPL volunteers, then propose a short AI module to CLOUD901 staff, citing the White Station HS precedent. A Teen Innovation Center branch (e.g., Cordova, nearest to Collierville) is a good second site.
- CLOUD901 seems to have no dedicated AI programming, so a teen AI workshop would fill a gap rather than duplicate one. This is based only on the Basics page and calendar snippets.
- The Memphis Library Foundation sponsors CLOUD901 programs, so it is the logical funder to approach once a Collierville pilot shows results.

### Gaps
- No current CLOUD901 staff roster or verified 2026 hours. Hours conflict across sources.
- No formal MPL partner-program proposal process found. It appears to go through staff, volunteer services, or outreach.
- Did not confirm whether MPL allows under-18 volunteers to lead programs, or what age minimum applies to mentor background checks.
- Did not check the computers and Wi-Fi available at CLOUD901 or Teen Innovation Centers in 2026.

## 3. Other local orgs doing teen coding/AI/STEM

### Takeaway
CodeCrew is the main Memphis youth coding nonprofit and already teaches AI, making it the most important partner or reference. It also has a working relationship with MPL. The University of Memphis hosts the West Tennessee STEM Hub. Collierville High School offers AP CS Principles and cybersecurity dual enrollment, but I found no AI course. No Girls Who Code or Hack Club chapters in the Memphis area could be confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- CodeCrew (Memphis nonprofit): after-school classes in coding, robotics, AI, VR, and cybersecurity, split by grade band. A Data Lab does project-based work with real data and predictive models. Fall 2026 after-school registration was on Eventbrite. Its annual hackathon (10th annual per Daily Memphian) has had AI themes ("AI for My City," 2024) — [Eventbrite Fall 2026](https://www.eventbrite.com/e/codecrew-after-school-fall-2026-registration-1993338930400); [Daily Memphian](https://dailymemphian.com/article/45203/memphis-business-codecrew-hosting-10th-annual-hackathon); [Local Memphis](https://localmemphis.com/article/news/local/memphis-code-crew-hackathon-2024-challenges-students-to-build-apps-involving-ai-that-impact-memphis/522-2a322cc9-8677-4d85-86ba-924a525475a8); [Kars4Kids profile](https://www.kars4kidsgrants.org/our-grantees/codecrew-closes-the-tech-gap-for-underserved-youth/)
- Black Girls Code listed Memphis among its 2026 summer camp cities (game design theme, ages 10+) — [EdTech Innovation Hub](https://edtechinnovationhub.com/news/black-girls-code-expands-summer-ai-and-game-design-camps-across-us)
- All Star Code: free six-week virtual program (Jul 6-Aug 14, 2026) for Black male teens, including AI literacy — [Defender Network](https://defendernetwork.com/news/local-state/all-star-code-seeks-black-male-teens-for-free-program/)

The hub runs a STEM Lending Library of kits for West TN teachers and a STEM Ambassadors program. It hosted the Innovations in STEM teacher conference in June 2026. UofM also promotes AI work ("PolytechnicUofM") — [UofM STEM Hub/SETWC](https://www.memphis.edu/setwc/k-12_outreach/); [2026 Innovations in STEM](https://www.memphis.edu/herff/news/2026_innovations_in_stem_conference.php); [Herff AI news](https://www.memphis.edu/herff/news/how_polytechnicuofm_is_leading_tennessees_ai_revolution.php); [TSIN 2012](https://www.tsin.org/news/2012/new-tsin-stem-school-and-regional-hub-open-in-west-tennessee)

- Junior Achievement of Memphis runs "Startup Park by FedEx" (2022 announcement) — [Memphis Chamber](https://members.memphischamber.com/news/details/junior-achievement-to-launch-startup-park-by-fedex-06-06-2022)
- Greater Memphis Chamber runs a STEM teacher externship. International Paper is among the host companies — [Memphis Chamber](https://memphischamber.com/blog/press-release/memphis-companies-host-teachers-this-summer-in-greater-memphis-chambers-teacher-externship-program/); [Daily Memphian](https://dailymemphian.com/article/36778/greater-memphis-chamber-of-commerce-stem-program-ted-townsend)

## 4. Local funding, grants, and sponsors

### Takeaway
The most realistic small-dollar funders for a Collierville pilot are the Collierville Rotary Foundation (annual grant round, deadline Sept 30 in 2026) and in-kind support from the library itself. The Collierville Education Foundation funds teachers only. For the Memphis expansion, the Memphis Library Foundation, the AutoZone Foundation, and the Community Foundation of Greater Memphis are the key targets.

### Cited Findings
- Collierville Rotary Foundation: 2026 grant applications were due Sept 30, 2026, and 2025 recipients were ineligible in 2026. The form lists a contact person — [Rotary application form](https://tally.so/r/2E9qDg)
- Collierville Education Foundation: founded 1996 and has given more than $2M. Grants go directly to Collierville Schools teachers. It gave a record $225,000 in one cycle — [Collierville Schools news](https://www.colliervilleschools.org/apps/news/article/1858620); [Grantable](https://grantable.co/funders/collierville-education-foundation-morg_e20f055eb1dc4f47afcbbed8960c54d8)
- Collierville Partners in Education exists as a nonprofit (details not reviewed) — [Grantable](https://grantable.co/nonprofits/collierville-partners-in-education-morg_05435f52d82d4116b5c25102fd43e66b)
- Community Foundation of Greater Memphis is the largest Memphis education funder by total giving and offers "Give 365 Memphis" grants — [Instrumentl](https://www.instrumentl.com/foundations/tennessee/memphis/education); [Shareway Give 365](https://www.theshareway.com/grant/community-foundation-94693-give-365-memphis-grants)
- AutoZone Foundation funds Greater Memphis nonprofits; education and youth development are focus areas — [Shareway](https://www.theshareway.com/grant/autozone-foundation-22816-greater-memphis-area-grants)
- International Paper Foundation is listed among Memphis education funders (about $3.4M) — [Instrumentl](https://www.instrumentl.com/foundations/tennessee/memphis/education)
- Memphis Library Foundation sponsors CLOUD901 programs (Section 2).

### Inferences
- Most of these funders give to 501(c)(3) organizations, not individuals. Running the workshop under the library (or under a fiscal sponsor such as the Friends of the Library or the Memphis Library Foundation) is likely needed to receive grants.
- A free AI workshop can be low-cost: library space, participants' own devices, and free AI tiers. A first pilot may need only small sponsorships for snacks, printing, or certificates, from Rotary, Kiwanis, or local businesses.
- Teacher-only grants from the Collierville Education Foundation could flow through a CHS teacher sponsor.

### Gaps
- No current 2026 info found on Collierville Kiwanis, Germantown community foundations, ServiceMaster, or FedEx youth STEM grant programs.
- Nothing found on Friends of the Burch Library funding.
- Grant cycles listed may be dated. Confirm on each funder's site.

## 5. Demographics and digital-divide context

### Takeaway
Collierville is affluent and almost fully connected: about 99% of households have a computer and about 97% have broadband. Shelby County overall is around 90% broadband subscription, with a much larger historic gap inside Memphis city. Expanding to Memphis means planning for loaner devices and in-library internet, not BYOD.

### Cited Findings
- Collierville (ACS 2019-2023): 98.7% of households have a computer and 97.4% have a broadband subscription — [Census QuickFacts](https://census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/colliervilletowntennessee/PST045223). A third-party summary gives 1.9% with no internet access — [City-Stats](https://city-stats.com/tennessee/collierville/utilities)
- Collierville median household income estimates conflict: about $119K-$134K across third-party sites — [DataUSA](https://datausa.io/profile/geo/collierville-tn); [Statistical Atlas](https://statisticalatlas.com/place/Tennessee/Collierville/Household-Income)
- Shelby County (ACS 2020-2024): 89.6% of households have a broadband subscription — [Census QuickFacts Shelby County](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/csv/shelbycountytennessee/COM100224)
- Memphis city historically: 126,428 of 256,973 households (about half) lacked broadband in 2016 data (NDIA, dated) — [Memphis Flyer 2018](https://memphisflyer.com/NewsBlog/archives/2018/06/14/report-half-of-memphis-homes-not-connected-to-broadband). Low-income Memphians receive worse internet service for the same price — [MLK50 2023](https://mlk50.com/2023/03/16/low-income-memphians-receive-worse-internet-service-for-the-same-price/). The state allocated $6M to 10 Shelby County organizations for broadband adoption — [Local Memphis](https://www.localmemphis.com/article/news/community/six-million-dollars-going-to-10-organizations-to-address-broadband-expansion/522-ed442569-e0e9-4e63-8281-5f36a1f6b0a7). Memphis has been cited among cities most affected by the digital divide — [BroadbandNow](https://broadbandnow.com/report/25-cities-most-impacted-by-the-digital-divide)

### Inferences
- The Collierville pilot can assume nearly all participants have devices and home internet. Homework between sessions is feasible.
- For Memphis branches, design so all work happens in-session on library or loaner devices, with browser-based tools and no installs. Avoid tools that need paid accounts or home connectivity. Partnering with CodeCrew or the Memphis Library Foundation could help supply devices.

### Gaps
- No current (2023+) Memphis-city-only broadband or computer figures were pulled directly from Census. The roughly 50% figure is from 2016. A Memphis city QuickFacts lookup is needed for a current comparison.
- No teen-specific device-access data for MSCS students.
