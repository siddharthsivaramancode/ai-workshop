# Session 1: Kickoff — From zero to a live URL

**Goal:** every teen leaves with (1) a basic understanding of how an AI chatbot works, (2) one example of the AI being confidently wrong, and (3) a page live on the internet.

**Deck:** [`decks/session-01-kickoff.pptx`](decks/session-01-kickoff.pptx). The speaker notes on each slide hold the script.

## Before the session

- [ ] Get every teen's parent permission form back, and confirm the library is holding them. The form names GitHub, GitHub Pages and ChatGPT.
- [ ] Email families 3–5 days ahead: "Create a GitHub account (13+) and a ChatGPT account (with your parent's permission) before Tuesday. Bring a charged laptop." This saves about 20 minutes on the day.
- [ ] The adult sponsor rehearses the Claude Code demo at least once, using the prompt in the deck (slide 9). Have a recording ready in case the Wi-Fi fails.
- [ ] Test the projector and Wi-Fi. Have the library's loaner laptops ready for anyone without one.
- [ ] Print: sign-in sheet, the 4-habits card, the safety rules, and prompt-journal sheets.
- [ ] Prepare the Checkpoint questions (slide 12), and look up the correct answers yourself from official sources the day before.

## Run of show (120 min)

| Time | Slides | Block | Who leads |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | 1–3 | Welcome and icebreaker: "If you could build any website, what would it be?" | Leader A |
| 0:10–0:25 | 4–7 | **What is AI, really?** The "Guess the next word" game, then the 3-step explanation of how a chatbot works. | Leader B |
| 0:25–0:40 | 8–10 | **Live demo.** The adult sponsor runs Claude Code, which builds a site from one sentence and publishes it. Teens call out changes. | Adult sponsor |
| 0:40–0:55 | 11–13 | **AI Checkpoint: Confident ≠ Correct.** Pairs ask their AI 3 local questions and check the answers against real sources. | Leader A |
| 0:55–1:05 | 14–15 | The 4 habits and the safety rules | Leader B |
| 1:05–1:45 | 16–19 | **Ship a page:** set up your GitHub repo, have the AI help write `index.html`, turn on Pages, and get a live URL | Both leaders circulate |
| 1:45–1:55 | 20–21 | Wall of URLs: show everyone's live link on screen. Then explain what "shipped" means and walk through the 6-week map. | Leader A |
| 1:55–2:00 | 22 | First prompt-journal entry. Homework: bring 3 project ideas. | Leader B |

## If things go wrong

- **A teen has no GitHub account or no permission form:** they pair with someone and watch. They don't get an account that day. Follow up with the parent.
- **The Wi-Fi is too slow for the demo:** play the backup recording.
- **Pages doesn't go live right away:** it can take 1–2 minutes. Move on and come back to it. Netlify Drop is the backup: drag the folder onto its page.
- **A teen finishes early:** challenge them to add a button that does something, and to explain it back to you.

## Measure (for future grant applications)

- Attendance count
- How many teens left with a live URL (target: 80% or more)
- From the prompt journals: how many wrote down a specific AI mistake
