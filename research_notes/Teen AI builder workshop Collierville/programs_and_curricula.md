# Existing AI Literacy and AI "Build-and-Ship" Programs for Teens (US, as of Nov 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-10. Sources are mostly search snippets and one direct page fetch (hackclub.com/clubs). Where a figure comes from a secondary source or conflicts across sources, that is flagged.

## 1. Free AI literacy curricula for grades 6–12: what exists, and what can non-teachers reuse?

### Takeaway
Several strong free curricula exist. Day of AI (MIT RAISE), CodeAI (formerly Code.org), Experience AI (Raspberry Pi Foundation + Google DeepMind), Common Sense Education, Stanford CRAFT, and the AI4K12 "Five Big Ideas" framework are the core set. Most are free, but the licenses are usually NonCommercial and often NoDerivatives. A free library workshop can *use* them with attribution, but in several cases it cannot publish remixed versions. The most current anchor framework is the EC/OECD AI Literacy Framework, finalized June 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Day of AI / MIT RAISE**
- In August 2026 (dated Aug 19), Day of AI launched what it calls the nation's first free, grade-by-grade PreK–12 AI literacy curriculum. It is free for educators, schools, and districts at dayofai.org/curriculum-resources, and a launch webinar was set for Sept 2. — [Day of AI news](https://dayofai.org/news/day-of-ai-launches-nations-first-free-grade-by-grade-prek-12-ai-literacy-curriculum)
- MIT RAISE developed it with a spiral approach based on the UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Students. Registration unlocks lesson plans and optional free PD, and the lessons are reported to be Creative Commons-licensed (exact CC variant not confirmed). — [Day of AI teachers](https://dayofai.org/teachers); [MIT RAISE program overview](https://raise.mit.edu/engage-with-us/k-12/day-of-ai-pilot); [MIT Open Learning](https://openlearning.mit.edu/news/day-ai-teaches-k-12-students-about-artificial-intelligence-mit-way)
- Day of AI also promoted a free 2-day virtual cohort and a fall community of practice in 2026, plus an NSF-funded free program for US high school educators. — [Day of AI / MIT RAISE national movement](https://dayofai.org/news/day-of-ai-and-mit-raise-launch-national-movement-to-empower-students-and-educators-in-the-ai-age-3)
- Note: I found no source that uses the acronym "RAICA". The "MIT RAICA" in the assignment is most likely MIT RAISE (Responsible AI for Social Empowerment and Education).

**CodeAI (Code.org rebranded, June 2026)**
- In June 2026, Code.org rebranded as "CodeAI". — [code.org/codeai](https://code.org/codeai); [codeorg-is-now-codeai](https://code.org/codeorg-is-now-codeai)
- **AI Foundations** is a free intro high school CS course (grades 9–12) that integrates AI, data science, cybersecurity, and programming. It comes in full-year, semester, or unit formats. A new unit, "Designing and Building with AI", completes the course, whose first semester has been in classrooms since May 2025. — [AI Foundations](https://code.org/curriculum/artificial-intelligence-foundations); [CodeAI launch](https://code.org/codeai-launches-ai-foundations-and-ai-discoveries)
- **AI Discoveries** is a newer, lower-grade pathway. — [AI Discoveries](https://code.org/curriculum/artificial-intelligence-discoveries)
- **How AI Works** is a free video series with short lessons (vocabulary + reflection question per video). It is well suited to 10-minute opening segments in a workshop. — [How AI Works](https://code.org/ai/how-ai-works)
- **Exploring Generative AI** covers how gen-AI works, creating with it, and its societal and ethical implications. — [Exploring Generative AI](https://code.org/curriculum/generative-ai)
- CodeAI supported the EC/OECD AI Literacy Framework. — [edtechinnovationhub](https://edtechinnovationhub.com/news/european-commission-and-oecd-set-19-ai-literacy-competences-for-schools)

**Experience AI (Raspberry Pi Foundation + Google DeepMind)**
- This is a free course. "Foundations of AI" is six lessons for ages 11–14, and challenge resource packs can be used anywhere in the world. — [experience-ai.org](https://experience-ai.org/); [Raspberry Pi blog](https://www.raspberrypi.org/blog/experience-ai-launch-lessons/)
- **License: CC BY-NC-ND 4.0** (per educator documents). You can share it with attribution, but not commercially and not in modified form. — [Experience AI educator doc](https://experience-ai.org/en/drive_resources/137.docx)
- A 2026 impact report exists (not reviewed in detail). — [Experience AI Impact report 2026](https://static.raspberrypi.org/files/about/Experience_AI_Impact_report_2026.pdf)

**Common Sense Education: AI Literacy Lessons, Grades 6–12**
- These are short lessons, mostly 15–20 minutes, plus one 50-minute lesson for grades 9–12. Topics: how AI is trained, chatbots, AI bias, algorithms and online content, facial recognition, and whether AI use counts as plagiarism. A free account is required. — [Colorado DOE listing](https://www.cde.state.co.us/computerscience/computer-science-resource-bank/detail/commonsenseailessons612); [Common Sense Education](https://www.commonsense.org/education/)
- License, as reported by a district re-host (secondary): shareable with attribution, noncommercial use only, no remixing. One district copy was last updated in October 2023, so the content may be dated. — [Palm Beach Schools page](https://sites.google.com/palmbeachschools.org/techsafe/secondary-ai-lessons)
- Common Sense also publishes an "AI Toolkit for Families", which ALA's PLA links for patron training. — [ALA PLA Patron Training](https://www.ala.org/pla/initiatives/ai/patrons)

**Stanford CRAFT (Classroom-Ready Resources About AI For Teaching)**
- CRAFT is a free, "continuously growing" collection of adaptable high-school AI literacy resources from Stanford GSE and HAI (led by Victor Lee). It is designed to slot into any subject (math, science, history, art) and was co-developed with teachers. — [craft.stanford.edu](https://craft.stanford.edu); [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/bringing-ai-literacy-high-schools)
- I did not verify the license or the current lesson count.

**AI4K12 (AAAI/CSTA)**
- The "Five Big Ideas" are Perception, Representation & Reasoning, Learning, Natural Interactions, and Societal Impact. Grade-band progression charts are available, and the posters are free to print. — [ai4k12.org](https://ai4k12.org/); [gradeband charts](https://ai4k12.org/gradeband-progression-charts/); [CSTA AI Priorities](https://csteachers.org/AI-Priorities-for-All-K-12-Students-Report-from-CSTA-AI4K12)

**aiEDU**
- aiEDU offers free AI literacy resources. Its 10-week HS course (grades 9–12) is listed as "In Development · 2026–2027", so it may not be available yet, and pricing is not stated. — [aiEDU educator resources](https://www.aiedu.org/educator-resources-1)

**Anthropic (Claude) education materials**
- Anthropic Academy offers free AI Fluency courses, including "AI Fluency for Students", "AI Fluency for Educators", "Teaching AI Fluency", and "AI Fluency for pK-12 Educators" (built with Teach For America). They are hosted on Skilljar and Coursera, with no payment needed. — [Anthropic Academy pK-12](https://anthropic.skilljar.com/ai-fluency-for-pk12-educators); [Coursera blog](https://blog.coursera.org/anthropic-launches-five-free-courses-on-coursera-to-help-build-ai-fluency/); [EducatorsTechnology](https://www.educatorstechnology.com/2026/01/free-ai-courses-for-educators-what-anthropic-academy-offers.html)
- **Claude for Teachers** launched July 14, 2026 and gives verified US K-12 educators free Claude access (sign up by June 30, 2027 for a year). A schools/districts offering followed on Aug 28, 2026. — [Anthropic: Claude for Teachers](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-for-teachers); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/anthropic-claude-for-teachers/)
- Course counts conflict across secondary sources (13 vs 17 courses).

**Frameworks**
- The EC/OECD AI Literacy Framework (final) was published June 18, 2026. It has 19 competences across engaging with, creating with, managing, and shaping AI, with primary and secondary classroom examples. It is non-binding, will inform PISA 2029 Media & AI Literacy, and drew on 2,000+ stakeholders in 100 countries (per secondary coverage). Final publication slipped from the "early 2026" target. — [edtechinnovationhub](https://edtechinnovationhub.com/news/european-commission-and-oecd-set-19-ai-literacy-competences-for-schools); [ailiteracyframework.org](https://ailiteracyframework.org/blog/new-ai-literacy-framework-to-equip-youth-in-an-age-of-ai/); [EU Digital Skills & Jobs](https://digital-skills-jobs.europa.eu/en/latest/news/new-ai-literacy-framework-common-reference-integrating-ai-literacy-education)
- The UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Students underpins Day of AI. — [MIT RAISE](https://raise.mit.edu/engage-with-us/k-12/day-of-ai-pilot)

### Inferences
- For the critical-AI half of the workshop, the easiest borrowable building blocks are Common Sense's 15–20 minute lessons (bias, training, chatbots), Code.org/CodeAI "How AI Works" videos, and Day of AI's new grade-banded lessons. The EC/OECD 19 competences (or AI4K12's Five Big Ideas) can serve as a one-page learning-goals checklist.
- Many of these resources are licensed NC-ND (Experience AI, reportedly Common Sense). Teen volunteers should link to or present the original materials with attribution rather than publish edited versions. A free library program is noncommercial, so classroom-style use appears fine.
- Most of these materials are written for teachers in school settings and assume 45–60 minute class periods. They need trimming for a library drop-in format.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the exact CC licenses for Day of AI, CRAFT, CodeAI, or Common Sense's official page. CodeAI/Code.org historically used CC BY-NC-SA, but that was not verified for 2026.
- I found no Google- or Microsoft-specific teen AI literacy curricula in this pass. Experience AI is the Google DeepMind partnership. ISTE materials were not researched, and neither were OpenAI's education materials.
- I could not determine whether non-teacher (student volunteer) accounts can register for Day of AI or Common Sense. Both appear to require only a free educator-style account.

## 2. Programs that teach teens to build and ship software with AI tools; what Hack Club offers clubs

### Takeaway
Hack Club is the closest existing match for teen-led "ship a real project" programs. It offers YSWS ("You Ship, We Ship") reward programs, up to $100 Club Starter funding, a clubs shop, free web IDE (Spaces), and HCB fiscal sponsorship (free to apply, 7% fee). However, the big 2025 events (Summer of Making, Shipwrecked) have ended, and their 2026 successors need checking. **Claude's consumer product requires users to be 18+**, which is a critical constraint for a workshop built around Claude with 13–17 year olds.

### Cited Findings
**Hack Club clubs (hackclub.com/clubs, fetched Nov 2026)**
- Club Starter: "up to $100 in funding" for club promotional materials. — [hackclub.com/clubs](https://hackclub.com/clubs/)
- Current club-facing YSWS programs listed on the page:
  - Boba Drops: host a workshop and make a website, get free boba.
  - Swirl: build an HTML/CSS site with a unique feature, get free ice cream.
  - Toppings: CSS enhancements.
  - Waffles: build a JS website.
  - Sprig: ship a game, get a Sprig console.
  - TerminalCraft: build a terminal app, get a hardware/microcontroller grant.
  - Downscale: PICO-8 game, get a club grant.
  - FuseRing.
  - Clubs Shop: Raspberry Pis, stickers, and shirts, bought with coins earned through YSWS.
  - Spaces: a free web IDE that can host and collaborate.
  - A club perks toolbox.
  — [hackclub.com/clubs](https://hackclub.com/clubs/)
- YSWS programs are generally open to ages 13–18. Payouts are per "weighted grant", and individual program grants can be small (e.g., Hackvault grants of $30–65 in April 2026). — [Hack Club YSWS spend tracker](https://ysws-true-spend.hackclub.com/programs/ysws-hackvault.html)
- Hack Club describes itself as a nonprofit network of 60,000+ teens. — [Shipwrecked info](https://shipwrecked.hackclub.com/info)
- **Ended/outdated:** Summer of Making 2025 has ended, and the site points to "Stardance", described as the largest STEM event of the summer for ages 13–18 with 71,000+ teens. Shipwrecked last ran Aug 8–11, 2025 (Boston Harbor island; earned via 60 hours / 4 projects on "The Bay"). I found no 2026 Shipwrecked. Arcade and High Seas were earlier (2024–25) programs and are presumably over. — [summer.hackclub.com](https://summer.hackclub.com/); [stardance.hackclub.com](https://stardance.hackclub.com/); [Shipwrecked](https://shipwrecked.hackclub.com/info)

**HCB fiscal sponsorship**
- HCB is free to apply, with a 7% fee on revenue. It is for teens 13–18 leading mission-driven, nonprofit-qualifying projects; for-profit and political projects are excluded. Projects become fiscally sponsored projects of The Hack Foundation (a 501(c)(3)), so donations are tax-deductible and the project can receive grants. All funds must flow through HCB. HCB is not a bank. — [hackclub.com/hcb](https://hackclub.com/hcb/); [HCB help: who can apply](https://help.hcb.hackclub.com/en/articles/14451842-who-can-apply-for-fiscal-sponsorship); [Fiscal Sponsor Directory](https://fiscalsponsordirectory.org/the-hack-foundation/)

**Girls Who Code**
- The free virtual **Pathways** program has AI + Society, Data Science + AI, Web Dev, Game Design, and Cybersecurity tracks, and no experience is required. It ran a summer 2026 session (Jun 29–Aug 14) and a fall session (Oct 19–Dec 4, 2026). Spring 2027 is open to grades 9–12, with priority deadline Oct 28. It is for girls and non-binary students. — [GWC Zendesk](https://girlswhocode.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/51581789285275-What-is-the-Girls-Who-Code-Pathways-Program); [opportunitiesforyouth](https://opportunitiesforyouth.org/2026/07/31/girls-who-code-pathways-2026-free-virtual-summer-program-to-learn-ai-coding-build-tech-for-good/)

**Tool age restrictions (critical)**
- **Claude: users must be 18+** per Anthropic support. A secondary report says Anthropic locks accounts it identifies as under-18. — [Anthropic support (age)](https://support.anthropic.com/ru/articles/8129614-есть-ли-возрастные-ограничения-для-использования-claude); [aiproductivity.ai (secondary)](https://aiproductivity.ai/news/anthropic-enforces-age-policy-under-18-lockouts/)
- Replit: under-13s are prohibited. A secondary summary says ages 13–17 may use it with parental consent. — [Conduct Atlas summary of Replit ToS](https://conductatlas.com/platform/replit/replit-terms-of-service/age-restrictions-and-minor-user-provisions/)
- Lovable/Bolt: no age-policy information was found.

**Other models**
- WWT blog "From prompt to published: ten students, one day and a live URL" is a one-day student vibe-coding event model (not read in full). — [WWT](https://www.wwt.com/blog/from-prompt-to-published-ten-students-one-day-and-a-live-url)

### Inferences
- The age rule is the biggest design constraint. Under-18 teens cannot have their own Claude accounts, so the student leads (if under 18) and their participants likely can't sign up for Claude directly. Options to check include:
  - an adult/library-held account used in demos;
  - Claude for Teachers or district access via a sponsoring educator (verify whether student use is permitted);
  - teen-allowed tools (Replit with parental consent, Hack Club Spaces, Google AI Studio or GitHub Copilot under their own teen terms; terms unverified).
- Hack Club's model (ship a project, get a tangible reward, with a public showcase) maps directly onto goal (a). Starting a Hack Club club at the library could unlock the $100 starter funding, YSWS prizes, and later HCB if the students want to accept donations.
- Hack Club YSWS requirements typically include a public repo and a deployed demo URL. That doubles as a "shipped" bar for the workshop.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm Hack Club's flagship 2026 programs beyond Stardance and the clubs page list, or whether any YSWS specifically targets AI-assisted coding.
- I did not research CoderDojo / Code Club (Raspberry Pi Foundation), Replit/Lovable/Bolt education offerings, or official Anthropic student/club programs (e.g., campus ambassador programs, which are university-level).
- I could not verify whether Claude for Teachers allows supervised student use.

## 3. What public libraries run for teen AI programming

### Takeaway
Many US libraries ran one-off teen AI sessions in 2026, including several explicitly "vibe coding" ones. Multi-week, teen-led, ship-a-project programs appear rare, which suggests a gap the workshop could fill. ALA has 2026 AI guidance (a harm-reduction, no-shaming lens) but no dedicated teen AI toolkit was found. I found no documented outcome data.

### Cited Findings
- East Lansing Public Library ran "Teens Vibe Coding for the Community" (with MSU Extension & 4-H), in which teens identify a local issue and build a functional app. — [ELPL event](https://elpl.bibliocommons.com/events/69b20c3ab48451fc3c084cc5)
- Weston Public Library ran "Learn about Vibe Coding" for ages 13+, bring your own laptop (spring 2026). — [Weston PL](https://westonpl.librarycalendar.com/event/learn-about-vibe-coding-5915)
- Palo Alto City Library ran "Vibe Coding 101: Build Your Own AI Journaling Tool" (May 30, 2026; adult-focused, no experience needed). — [Palo Alto](https://www.paloalto.gov/Events-Directory/Library/Vibe-Coding-101-Build-Your-Own-AI-Journaling-Tool)
- Pikes Peak Library District ran an AI Prompt Engineering Workshop for ages 13–18. — [PPLD](https://ppld.librarymarket.com/event/ai-prompt-engineering-workshop-518243)
- Other examples:
  - Austin Public Library: Tween Code Club, AI.
  - Virginia Beach PL: Exploring AI Through Coding.
  - Colonie Library: Introduction to Python and AI, a teen-led series for grades 6–8 (a peer-led precedent).
  — [Austin PL](https://library.austintexas.gov/event/youth-programs-workshops/tween-code-club-artificial-intelligence-7779311); [VBPL](https://vbpl.librarymarket.com/exploring-ai-through-coding); [Colonie](https://events.colonielibrary.org/event/introduction-python-and-ai-32987)
- ALA 2026 "Guidance on the Use of AI in Libraries" recommends teaching AI literacy through a public-service, harm-reduction lens. It says programs should avoid shaming people who are curious about, using, required to use, skeptical of, or avoiding AI. — [ALA guidance](https://www.ala.org/tools/standards-and-guidelines/guidance-use-artificial-intelligence-libraries); [PDF](https://www.ala.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/ALA%20CD%2044.2%20AI%20Guidance%20Document%20-%20Final.pdf)
- ALA/PLA AI hub pages for staff and patron training link to Common Sense's AI Toolkit for Families and Elements of AI. ALA also held a "Future-Ready Youth: Preparing Our Patrons to Navigate the Age of AI" event, and ALSC sessions at ALA Annual 2026 covered teen AI interaction research and curriculum sources for library AI programming. — [PLA AI](https://www.ala.org/pla/initiatives/ai); [ALA Future-Ready Youth](https://www.ala.org/events/future-ready-youth-preparing-our-patrons-navigate-age-ai); [ALSC blog](https://www.alsc.ala.org/blog/tag/ai-literacy/); [ALA AI learning](https://www.ala.org/tools/ai-learning)
- An AASL/ALA Editions book: "Leveraging AI in School Libraries: From Basics to Best Practices". — [ALA AI learning](https://www.ala.org/tools/ai-learning)

### Inferences
- Pitch angle for the library: a multi-week, peer-led, ship-a-real-thing program aligns with ALA's 2026 guidance and is less common than one-off sessions. ELPL's "community problem → app" framing is a strong template.
- Framing the critical-AI content as harm reduction rather than "AI is bad/cheating" matches ALA guidance and will likely be more welcome to library staff.

### Gaps
- I found no documented outcome data (attendance, completion, learning gains) for any library teen AI program.
- YOUmedia (Chicago PL / Urban Libraries Council digital learning labs) AI programming was not found in this pass. YALSA-specific AI resources were not located.
- I found no Tennessee/Memphis-area library teen AI programs in this pass.

## 4. Evidence on what works in short teen AI literacy programs

### Takeaway
The evidence is thin but consistent. Short, hands-on, experiential AI literacy work beats lecture. The best controlled study, from France, found a single two-hour workshop improved how 8th–9th graders used and regulated chatbots on later tasks. Behavior, not self-reported attitudes, is the better outcome measure.

### Cited Findings
- France, controlled study, N=116, grades 8–9: a 2-hour AI literacy workshop given two days before LLM-supported science exercises led to better performance and more sophisticated regulation of LLM interactions vs. control. Self-reported attitudes did not predict problem-solving; behavioral metrics captured the effect. — [arXiv 2604.01955](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01955)
- A K-12 field experiment found an experiential AI lesson produced higher AI knowledge, higher AI readiness, and lower AI anxiety than a classical lesson. The authors note limited empirical work on hands-on approaches. — [WI 2024, AIS eLibrary](https://aisel.aisnet.org/wi2024/24)
- Australia, Years 7–10, pre/post workshop (205 pre / 163 post; no control evident): short-term gains in AI literacy and ethical understanding, and it highlighted how common deepfake creation and sharing is among students. — [arXiv 2601.22486](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.22486)
- Summer camp pilot, ages 12–15, N=18, four 2-hour sessions mixing plugged and unplugged activities: engaging without prior knowledge, with some indication that ethics instruction helped. — [ASEE paper](https://nemo.asee.org/public/conferences/374/papers/53357/download)
- A pilot had adolescents work on non-trivial tasks with ChatGPT specifically to expose its limits and reduce fear. — [arXiv 2307.01540](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.01540)
- A narrative review (2009–2024) concludes that AI literacy develops best when students actively manipulate and experiment with AI systems rather than passively consume theory. — [Constructing AI Literacy, CEEOL](https://www.ceeol.com/search/article-detail?id=1418321)
- The EC/OECD framework warns that adding AI literacy without time or training increases workload. This is relevant to volunteer capacity. — [edtechinnovationhub](https://edtechinnovationhub.com/news/european-commission-and-oecd-set-19-ai-literacy-competences-for-schools)

### Inferences
- The build track can deliver the critical track. Having teens catch AI-generated bugs, hallucinated APIs, insecure code, or biased outputs inside their own project is exactly the "experiment with the system" approach the evidence favors.
- Measure behavior (e.g., did they verify AI output or catch an error?) rather than relying only on attitude surveys.
- A single focused 2-hour critical-AI session early in the series has controlled-study support.

### Gaps
- Most studies are small pilots or pre/post designs, with few US samples and none in library or peer-led settings.
- I found no research specifically on teens learning "vibe coding" / AI-assisted software development and whether it builds or erodes understanding.
- I read only abstracts and snippets; effect sizes were not verified.
