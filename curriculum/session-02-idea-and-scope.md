# Session 2: Idea and scope — Big idea, small start

**Goal:** every teen leaves with (1) a one-sentence pitch, (2) a list of 3 features split into Must, Nice and Someday, (3) a first version of their Must feature that is live at their URL, and (4) a clear sense of what never goes into a prompt or onto their site.

**Deck:** [`slideshows/session-02-idea-and-scope.pptx`](../slideshows/session-02-idea-and-scope.pptx). The speaker notes on each slide hold the script.

## Before the session

- [ ] Print the sorting cards for the privacy game (slide 4), one set per pair. The 9 cards are listed on the slide.
- [ ] Print pitch sheets, each with the pitch formula, a box for Must / Nice / Someday, and room for a sketch.
- [ ] Put the planning prompt (slide 16) somewhere teens can copy it, such as a shared doc or a QR code.
- [ ] Check that everyone from session 1 has a live URL. If anyone doesn't, have a junior mentor help them for the first 10 minutes.
- [ ] Bring the sticky notes from session 1 that list everyone's dream sites.

## Answers to the privacy sort (slide 5)

| Safe | Depends | Not safe |
|---|---|---|
| Your favorite band | Your first name. It's fine to use, but a nickname is safer on a public site. | The name of your school |
| A homework question | Your pet's name. It's a common answer to password security questions. | A photo of a friend. They didn't agree to it. |
| Your project idea | Your city. Fine in general, but combined with other details it can identify you. | Your password |

## If things go wrong

- **A teen can't pick an idea:** give them the "flashcards for a class you're taking" default. It can be done in two weeks and they'll actually use it.
- **The idea is too big** (logins, a database, AI inside the app, multiplayer): keep the dream as their *Someday* column. Then ask: "What's the one screen that would prove it's cool?" Build that screen.
- **The AI writes 300 lines at once:** send them back to the planning prompt. Ask for step 1 only, in fewer than 40 lines.
- **A teen copies AI code without reading it:** have them do explain-back before the commit. Ask them to point at the line that makes X happen.

## Measure

- Attendance, and how many returned from session 1
- How many teens wrote a pitch and a Must / Nice / Someday list
- How many committed feature 1 and have it live
